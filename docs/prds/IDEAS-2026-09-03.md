## Synthetic Nuance in Clinical Eval Cases: What Survives Augmentation

**The question (Sep 1):** if we take a real longitudinal case and add synthetic complications to make frontier models fail, do we destroy (a) validity for the physician reviewer and (b) value for the lab buyer? One founder: modality consistency is sacred. The other: a small percentage of tweaking raises difficulty without breaking validity.

**What the evidence says.** Both founders are right, at different layers.

Serious benchmarks already do exactly this, with one hard rule: perturbations must be answer-preserving. Microsoft's [MedFuzz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06573) ([blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/medfuzz-exploring-the-llms-on-medical-challenge-problems/)) adversarially rewrites MedQA vignettes (adding distracting patient characteristics and social noise) under the constraint that the clinically correct answer does not change, then has clinicians confirm the edit did not alter ground truth. GPT-4 dropped from 90.2% to 85.4% on fuzzed items. That is the existence proof for the "slight tweaking" position: difficulty went up, validity held, because edits stayed in the presentation layer, never the findings layer.

Fully constructed cases are also market-accepted when physicians gate them. [HealthBench](https://openai.com/index/healthbench/) is 5,000 largely synthetically generated conversations, filtered for realism and self-consistency, with 262 physicians writing 48,562 rubric criteria ([paper](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/bd7a39d5-9e9f-47b3-903c-8b847ca650c7/healthbench_paper.pdf)). MedQA itself is constructed USMLE vignettes, and the [NBME Item-Writing Guide](https://www.nbme.org/educators/item-writing-guide) is essentially a distractor-augmentation manual: distractors must be plausible, homogeneous, and logically compatible, and the vignette must stay internally consistent. Med educators have added difficulty without breaking validity for decades; internal consistency is the invariant they never trade away.

But the highest-prestige diagnostic evals are real cases precisely because provenance is the value claim: [Kanjee et al., JAMA 2023](https://ai.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/AIp2300031) used 70 real NEJM CPC cases, now treated as a gold standard and reused across later studies. And on the buyer side, the synthetic-data literature warns of degradation and "synthetic trust" ([Lancet Digital Health via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12778113/), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-02869-0)); labs pay [Mercor](https://www.mercor.com/experts/healthcare/) $50-180/hr for physician-authored and physician-verified cases, not for machine-generated ones. This matches the Sep 2 YC research finding: labs are wary of synthetic data as such. What they buy is real provenance plus verified difficulty.

**Recommendation: a three-tier augmentation policy.**

- **Allowed (presentation layer):** MedFuzz-style edits on a real substrate. Add plausible comorbidities, medication lists, social history, red-herring but real lab values, and NBME-grade distractors, provided every finding remains concordant with the true diagnosis and the correct management does not change.
- **Forbidden (findings layer):** never alter or fabricate modality-level evidence: imaging, pathology, key labs. A brain-tumor case with a contradicting MRI fails internal consistency, which is the one rule NBME, HealthBench, and MedFuzz all keep. Modality consistency is sacred; the first founder wins that layer outright.
- **Provenance labeling:** every case carries `real | real-augmented | constructed`, with a stored diff of edits. Sell augmented cases as "real substrate, physician-verified augmentation," never as raw real data.

**Sign-off:** the case's assigned reviewer (the 1 in the 2+1 room from the task-pipeline PRD) attests two things in the existing per-case attestation flow: (1) internally consistent, I would accept this chart, and (2) the augmentation did not change the correct answer or management. A labeler cannot self-attest their own augmentation. Then the empirical difficulty gate (`empirical_difficulty.py`, Wilson lower bound) is the buyer-facing proof that the added nuance actually bought hardness; an augmented case that models still pass gets its edits rejected, not shipped.

**Build-sized next step:** add the `provenance` enum plus `augmentation_diff` field to the case schema, and one checkbox pair ("internally consistent" / "answer-preserving") to the existing reviewer attestation. Pilot on 20 already-measured real cases: augment, re-run the difficulty pipeline, and compare pass rates before writing any generator.

## The Verified Physician Card: what makes a badge worth showing off

**The founders' question.** We already ship a verified card (backend/asclepius/card.py: tokenized public URL, live-revocable, OG unfurl to a server-rendered 1200x630 PNG, seven whitelisted practice facts). What makes a card like this something physicians voluntarily share, what cheapens it, and which three mechanics should we build next?

**What the evidence says.** Status badges hold value through scarcity plus real verification. Doximity's checkmark works because it sits on genuine credential verification (medical-domain email, roughly three-day review, and a 182-point primary-source process for recruiting candidates) across a network claiming 85%+ of US physicians, which is why hospitals and recruiters treat it as identity infrastructure ([Doximity Talent Solutions](https://doximitytalentsolutions.com/the-doximity-difference), [Doximity registration](https://support.doximity.com/hc/en-us/articles/360047630573-How-to-Register-on-Doximity)). The negative case is X/Twitter: opening the checkmark to anyone with $8 destroyed its scarcity and inverted its meaning from "vouched for" to "paid for", spiking impersonation and turning the badge into a mild embarrassment ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2023/04/24/tech/musk-twitter-blue-check-mark), [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/technology/2023/04/27/paying-for-twitter-blue-has-ironically-robbed-verification-of-value/), [Section](https://www.sectionai.com/blog/how-did-twitters-blue-check-mark-go-from-status-symbol-to-total-embarrassment)). LinkedIn's badge shows the upside of scarce-but-free: LinkedIn reports verified members get about 60% more profile views, though independent analysis finds the badge matters less than photo and headline, so a badge amplifies a profile rather than replacing one ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1359065), [causal analysis](https://medium.com/@t8rohman/does-linkedins-verification-badge-really-boost-views-a-perspective-from-causal-inference-1cee867fc873)).

On sharing mechanics: Credly badges spread because of a one-click "Add to LinkedIn" flow that drops the credential permanently into the Licenses & Certifications section, each badge carrying a unique verification URL anyone can check ([Credly](https://support.credly.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021221491-How-can-I-add-my-badge-to-my-LinkedIn-profile-and-share-to-my-feed)). Spotify Wrapped and Strava show why people post at all: assets pre-sized for the destination (9:16 stories, square feed), a one-tap share with zero cropping friction, and content that gives the sharer something to say about themselves, not just something to look at ([NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/spotify-wrapped-marketing-strategy/), [Trophy](https://trophy.so/blog/how-to-build-wrapped-feature)). ORCID demonstrates durability without glamour: 3.7M+ researchers adopted a plain green iD because it is embedded in 500+ submission and grant workflows, meaning identity artifacts win when they are required or useful somewhere else ([UKSG Insights](https://insights.uksg.org/articles/10.1629/2048-7754.103), [Elsevier](https://www.elsevier.com/connect/ten-reasons-to-get-and-use-an-orcid-id)). Sermo is the counterexample that clarifies our position: verification without public identity (most members stay anonymous) builds a safe forum but no outward-facing status object and no recruiting flywheel ([Sermo](https://www.sermo.com/blog/insights/is-sermo-legit/)).

**Recommendation: the three mechanics worth building next.**

1. **"Add to LinkedIn" deep link** on the mint screen, Credly-style: prefill name "Verified Physician, Archangel Health", issuer, mint date, and the card URL as the credential URL. This puts a permanent, verifiable backlink on the one profile every colleague and recruiter already reads, and costs one URL template.
2. **A one-tap share-image kit**: add a 1080x1920 story-format render beside the existing 1200x630 OG render, from the same `card_payload` dict (a second layout function in card.py), with download buttons next to copy-link. Format-fit plus zero friction is the whole Wrapped lesson.
3. **Re-share moments from D4-safe counts**: milestone variants of the same card ("500 cases reviewed on Archangel"), counts and streaks only, never score or band. A card is shared once; milestones give physicians a reason to post again, and each post recruits.

**What NOT to do.** Never loosen or sell the checkmark, and never badge pre-approval accounts (the Twitter failure). No NPI, pedigree, or score on any surface (already locked). Skip wallet passes and image-only sharing for now: a PNG without our URL behind it is unverifiable and unrevocable, and the PRD rightly parked wallet exports.

**Build-sized next step.** Ship mechanic 1 this week: one anchor tag on the mint-success screen using LinkedIn's Add-to-Profile URL scheme pointing at `card_url(token)`, plus a test asserting the link renders only for approved, minted accounts. About 30 lines including the test.

## Keeping the community alive: humans set the tone, bots only fill silence

**The founders' question:** we built an automated community (specialty and city rooms, daily AI-generated event/research/news posts, a weekly poll, an Archangel admin persona, morning digests). Will it feel alive, or dead on arrival, and what do we personally have to do at launch?

**What the evidence says.** Professional communities that survived were gated, human, and founder-tended. Sermo and Doximity won by guaranteeing every member was a verified physician, which made peer content credible enough to change clinical opinion ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2012/03/sermo-vs-doximity-can-they-co-exist-in-fight-to-build-physician-only-networks/), [Sermo](https://www.sermo.com/blog/sermo-speaks/do-this-not-that-8-proven-ways-to-spark-sustain-physician-engagement/)); Doximity's early edge was a CEO who visibly answered member feedback. Blind's cold start was work-email verification plus concentrated seeding of a few companies until real insider talk took over ([HBS Digital case](https://aiinstitute.hbs.edu/platform-digit/submission/blind-building-and-scaling-anonymous-community/)). Reddit's founders personally submitted nearly all content for weeks under human-crafted usernames; the point was curated taste that set the tone, and it tapered as members took over ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-reddit-got-huge-tons-of-fake-accounts-2/)). The participation math is stable: roughly 1% post, 9% react, 90% lurk ([Nielsen's 90-9-1](https://hivebrite.io/glossary/90-9-1-rule/)), so at launch the founders ARE the 1%, and the goal is not converting lurkers but removing friction for the moment they have something to say ([PeepSo](https://peepso.com/understanding-the-90-9-1-rule-and-how-to-optimise-it/)).

The failure pattern is equally clear: feeds that are mostly synthetic kill the room. When members sense filler, they disengage, and the community dies in a self-fulfilling loop ([Wevolver on dead-internet dynamics](https://www.wevolver.com/article/combatting-the-dead-internet-theory-in-an-ai-world)). Meta deleted all 28 of its AI persona accounts in January 2025 after users called them creepy and unnecessary ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/01/03/business/meta-ai-accounts-instagram-facebook), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/meta-ai-insta-shuts-character-instagram-fb-accounts-user-outcry-rcna186177)). Physicians are a high-BS-detector audience; an "Archangel" persona wearing your photo but posting machine-written text is the Meta pattern with your faces on it.

**Recommendation: 2-week activation playbook.** Daily, both founders, 20 to 30 minutes each:

1. One personal post per founder per day, written by hand: what you saw building the product, a hard case that surprised you, a genuine question. Post via the admin composer, but the words are yours.
2. Reply to every member post within 2 hours during launch weeks. Zero-reply first posts are how rooms die.
3. DM every first-time poster same day ("great question, tagging Dr. X"), and name-check members in posts: one named-member spotlight per day (with permission).
4. One controversial-but-safe poll per week, clinical judgment calls with no right answer ("GLP-1 first-line in this presentation?"), not AI-picked news topics.
5. Concentrate, Blind-style: pick the 2 to 3 specialty rooms where you already have density and make those feel alive; ignore the long tail until rooms cross threshold organically.

**Throttle or keep, per the built automations (prd-community-activation.md):** Keep the morning newsletter exactly as built; its quiet-standdown (`send_for_member` returns quiet) is the right instinct. Keep events posts and the weekly poll (with the prose fallback). Keep the staff-only spotlight; it is internal. Throttle hard: daily AI research/news posts drop from daily-per-room to at most 2 to 3 per week, digest-style, and never into a room where a member posted in the last 24 hours. Retire the idea of the persona ever auto-posting; requirement 12 to 14 already makes it a manual staff composer, keep it that way and have founders sign posts with their own names.

**Build-sized next step:** extend the newsletter's standdown logic to the morning composer: before any bot post, compute the member-authored share of the room's last 20 messages; skip the post if members are active, and add a global env cap (`COMMUNITY_BOT_POSTS_PER_ROOM_PER_WEEK`, default 3). One function in `community/morning.py`, one config var, one test. The bot becomes a silence-filler that automatically gets quieter as the room gets real.

## Automated task routing: how expert marketplaces staged it, and a 4-stage ladder for Archangel

**The founders' question:** today an admin previews every case and hand-picks 2 labelers + 1 reviewer; profile depth now feeds a rank key below load and internal score. The meeting's end state is "automate task routing fully." What do we automate first, what stays human, and what triggers each step?

**What the evidence says.** Every platform that got this right automated *matching within an already-qualified pool* first and kept *qualification itself* human the longest. Gengo gates all work behind a two-stage test (machine-scored multiple choice, then a human-graded written test), holds a separate human-graded Pro exam for harder jobs, and only then lets routing be a simple claim-from-the-board mechanism among the qualified ([Gengo qualification](https://support.gengo.com/hc/en-us/articles/231441367-How-do-I-become-a-Gengo-translator), [quality levels](https://support.gengo.com/hc/en-us/articles/231438007-Our-Quality-Levels)). Toloka automates the *demotion* side: per-worker skill scores update from gold questions and post-verification, and rules auto-block workers whose skill drops below threshold ([task assignment survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.08501)). Surge keeps multi-stage vetting with live human work review even at ~50k contractors ([Surge vs Scale](https://averroes.ai/blog/surge-ai-vs-scale-ai)); Mercor automated the interview itself but still routes by score into projects, not open queues ([Mercor AI interview](https://talent.docs.mercor.com/support/ai-interview)). Radiology telereads are the closest analog to your 2+1 case unit: vRad and Smart-Assign auto-route studies on subspecialty, credentialing, schedule, and load, with priority overrides (stroke jumps every worklist) and turnaround alerts ([vRad platform](https://www.vrad.com/radiology-platform-solution/), [Smart-Assign](https://www.itnonline.com/content/automated-case-assignment-load-balancing-introduced-large-radiology-groups)). The failure mode when routing and QA automate too early is well documented at Remotasks/Outlier: automated rejections and silent filtering with no explanation bred distrust, gaming, and quality disputes ([Remotasks review](https://nodemaven.com/blog/remotasks-review/), [Scale AI criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_AI)). The lesson: automate assignment, never automate un-explained judgment about people.

**Recommendation: a 4-stage ladder.** This mirrors the measure-only-then-require pattern you already locked for the difficulty gate in `prd-task-pipeline.md`.

1. **Stage 0, tonight: manual routing, ranked shortlist.** Admin still picks, but the picker shows candidates pre-sorted by the rank key (specialty match, load, internal score, profile depth). Advance when: roster metrics ship (median seconds + per-physician kappa with n) and 25+ approved physicians.
2. **Stage 1: proposed slate, one-click approve.** System proposes the full 2+1 slate; admin approves or edits, and every edit is logged as an override with a reason. Advance when: override rate under ~20% across 4 consecutive weeks and 50+ cases/week (below that volume you learn nothing).
3. **Stage 2: auto-route the routine, human-gate the exceptions.** Slates auto-send when all three members are "proven" (5+ completed cases, kappa above floor at min n, load under cap, specialty pool of 3+). Exceptions still go to the admin queue: a physician's first 3 cases, thin specialties, kappa below floor, and all reassignments. Advance when: exception share under ~15% of volume and quality (pooled kappa, acceptance) flat vs Stage 1 for a month.
4. **Stage 3: full auto with monitoring.** Auto-routing plus stall-based auto-reassignment (turn the relay nudge on), priority jumps for urgent client orders, and admin dashboards + weekly spot audits instead of previews. Promotion to reviewer tier and any demotion stay human decisions with a written reason, forever. Realistic threshold: 150 to 200 active physicians, several hundred cases/week.

**Build-sized next step:** Stage 0 is one PR. Add a `GET /api/asclepius/admin/route_candidates?case_id=` endpoint that returns the ranked 2+1 shortlist (rank key already exists), render it in the routing modal, and add an `override` boolean + free-text reason to the send payload. That log is the dataset that decides when Stage 1 flips, exactly like the A3 written check gates the difficulty flag.
